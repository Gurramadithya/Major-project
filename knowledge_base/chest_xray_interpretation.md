# Chest X-Ray Interpretation Guide

## Normal Chest X-Ray Findings
- Clear lung fields without opacities
- Normal heart size (cardiothoracic ratio < 0.5)
- Sharp costophrenic angles
- Normal mediastinal silhouette
- Visible vascular markings

## Common Abnormalities

### Pneumonia
- **Lobar pneumonia**: Consolidation confined to one or more lobes
- **Bronchopneumonia**: Patchy consolidations, often bilateral
- **Interstitial pneumonia**: Reticular or nodular patterns
- Typical appearance: Air bronchograms, consolidation, opacity

### Pulmonary Edema
- **Cardiogenic**: Bat-wing distribution, Kerley B lines, cardiomegaly
- **Non-cardiogenic**: Diffuse ground-glass opacities, normal heart size
- Typical appearance: Bilateral infiltrates, pleural effusions

### Pleural Effusion
- Blunted costophrenic angle
- Meniscus sign
- Opacification of lung base
- May be unilateral or bilateral

### Atelectasis
- Volume loss in lung region
- Crowding of ribs
- Elevation of hemidiaphragm
- Mediastinal shift toward affected side

### Pneumothorax
- Absence of lung markings in periphery
- Visible visceral pleural line
- Hyperinflation of contralateral lung
- May cause mediastinal shift

## Systematic Approach
1. **Identify patient and view details**
2. **Assess technical quality** (rotation, penetration, inspiration)
3. **Check for life-threatening conditions** (pneumothorax, tension pneumothorax)
4. **Examine bones and soft tissues**
5. **Evaluate mediastinum and heart**
6. **Assess lung fields systematically**
7. **Review pleural spaces**
8. **Check tubes and lines** (if present)

## Red Flags Requiring Immediate Attention
- Large pneumothorax (>15%)
- Tension pneumothorax (mediastinal shift, tracheal deviation)
- Wide mediastinum (aortic dissection)
- Free air under diaphragm (perforated viscus)
- Massive pleural effusion

## Specialist Referral
Consult a **radiologist** for:
- Complex or ambiguous findings
- Atypical presentations
- Comparison with prior studies
- Advanced imaging recommendations
