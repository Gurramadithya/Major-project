# Pneumonia: Clinical Guide

## Overview
Pneumonia is an infection that inflames the air sacs in one or both lungs. The air sacs may fill with fluid or pus, causing cough with phlegm, fever, chills, and difficulty breathing.

## Types
- **Community-acquired pneumonia (CAP)**: Acquired outside of hospitals or healthcare settings
- **Hospital-acquired pneumonia (HAP)**: Acquired during hospital stay
- **Ventilator-associated pneumonia (VAP)**: Acquired while on mechanical ventilation

## Common Symptoms
- Cough with phlegm
- Fever, chills
- Shortness of breath
- Chest pain
- Fatigue
- Nausea, vomiting, or diarrhea

## Risk Factors
- Age (65+ or young children)
- Chronic lung diseases (COPD, asthma)
- Weakened immune system
- Smoking
- Recent respiratory infection

## Diagnosis
- Physical examination
- Chest X-ray
- Blood tests
- Sputum culture
- Pulse oximetry

## Treatment
- **Bacterial pneumonia**: Antibiotics (amoxicillin, azithromycin, levofloxacin)
- **Viral pneumonia**: Antiviral medications, supportive care
- Supportive care: Rest, fluids, fever reducers
- Severe cases: Hospitalization, oxygen therapy, IV antibiotics

## Prevention
- Pneumococcal vaccine
- Flu vaccine
- Good hygiene (hand washing)
- Not smoking
- Avoiding sick individuals

## Specialist Referral
Consult a **pulmonologist** for:
- Severe or recurrent pneumonia
- Complications (pleural effusion, abscess)
- Underlying lung disease
- Immunocompromised patients
